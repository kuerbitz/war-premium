    3 · The bond side: term premium and duration

    Term premium: the extra yield investors demand to hold a 10-year Treasury instead of rolling over short-term bills. Modified duration: roughly the % a 10-year Treasury's price falls if its yield rises 1 percentage point. Same window as chart 1.

    4 · The test: do term-premium moves line up with defense stocks?

    Each dot is one week. Across: how much the term premium changed that week. Up: how much the defense series beat (or trailed) the S&P 500 that week, as a log return. If war-driven borrowing pushed up the term premium and defense stocks together, the dots would slope upward. Weekly changes are used instead of levels so two unrelated trends can't fake a relationship.

    Method & sources

    Log return (CCR)
    r = ln(Pend / Pstart), shown ×100 as %. Log returns add across time, which is why they're used throughout.
    Modified duration of the 10-year Treasury
    Computed from the 10-year par yield y. A par bond's coupon equals its yield and its price is 100, which gives a closed form (20 semiannual periods): Dmod = (1/y) × [1 − (1 + y/2)−20] years. Example: y = 4.00% → (1/0.04) × [1 − 1.02−20] = 8.18 years.
    Volatility, Sharpe ratio, beta, alpha
    Volatility = population standard deviation of daily log returns × √252 (Excel STDEV.P). Sharpe = (annualized return − rf) / annualized volatility, where rf is the average 3-month T-bill yield over the same window, converted from its bond-equivalent quote to a continuously compounded rate: rf = 2 × ln(1 + y/2). Beta = Cov(r, rIVV) / Var(rIVV) (Excel SLOPE); alpha = (mean r − β × mean rIVV) × 252 (Excel INTERCEPT × 252).
    Term premium
    Kim–Wright (2005) three-factor model estimate, published by the Federal Reserve Board. It is a model estimate, not a market price, and is released with a lag of about a week.
    Weekly sampling
    For each series the last available observation in each calendar week (Mon–Sun) is used. A weekly change is only computed between back-to-back weeks.
    Line of best fit
    Ordinary least squares (OLS): slope = Σ(x−x̄)(y−ȳ) / Σ(x−x̄)², intercept = ȳ − slope·x̄, R² = correlation². R² is the share of the week-to-week variation in the defense series' excess return that term-premium changes explain.
    Event dates
    Chosen from news reports before looking at the market data. Each window starts at the last close before the event, so the event's own reaction is included.
